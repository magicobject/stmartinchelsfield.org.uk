    Churchyard

    About four acres of peaceful, much-loved ground around St Martin's.

    A peaceful haven

    Our churchyard is about four acres, and it is a peaceful haven. It has been a sought-after resting place for parishioners for hundreds of years. The names on the war memorial in the porch, and the scattering of war graves, are remembered each year on Remembrance Sunday.

    Different areas reflect the season: there are drifts of snowdrops in January; daffodils, primroses, cowslips and ladies smock in March; a sea of cow-parsley in June; and a well-kept lawn for summer weddings. In the autumn the whole churchyard is carpeted with leaves. In July 2003 a wild-plant botanist identified 61 different species of wild flowers here.

    The churchyard is visited all through the day, by people tending graves (some travel many miles) and by walkers: local footpaths cross it, and the council publicises the "Walks", so hikers are often surprised to find the church open. A churchyard extension was completed in 2014. There are no exceptional monuments, just the Lords of the Manor and their people. The car park is unusually large.

    Looking after the churchyard

    All of this takes a great deal of upkeep. A contractor looks after the grounds in the summer, and we rely on volunteers for the rest: cutting the lawn used for weddings, keeping the paths and grass in order (with care, so that the wild flowers and wildlife are protected), and joining special clearance days for the tougher jobs. Many of the trees are being pruned and tended so that they stay healthy.

    Churchyard Working Party: we meet on the first Saturday of each month, from 9–11am, to keep our beautiful churchyard looking good. All are welcome, and if you can give a few hours here and there we will be very grateful. See the calendar for the dates, and Groups & Activities for our other groups.
